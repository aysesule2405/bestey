in the room of the feast, we talked of months
and renamed the simpler ones after simpler gods.

the silhouette that rest its head in my lap
was wrong, so thick that it wasn’t really a shadow

wasn’t the cheap love I sought, couldn’t fit
in my cartoon cup half-filled with vodka cran.

after many days of throwing blankets on the feast,
I sat down and pictured it, following me into a bathroom,

burning domestic appliances along the way. floating,
dragging, syrupy, locking the door behind us

like the boogeyman, touching my neck
and twisting me inside out.

I imagined it with diligence and such heart,
but it didn’t do the trick. nothing did.

I was not a prison girl who stretched out her stories
to welcome a yellower sun, nor was I the dreamer

I mistook myself for. I knew the silhouette would remain
a glitch, a glistening half-error, a promise, a green lit guess.

that night, I slept on the carpet alone, refrained
from fantasy. willful abstinence. no hand

on the back of my head. no hand to [redacted].
just the slow drag to the bathroom

to find a stiller floor.
